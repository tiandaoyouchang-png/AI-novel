# Artifact Contracts

## Authority order

1. Accepted chapter prose and explicit author decisions.
2. Accepted topic decision, planning Markdown, and continuity YAML.
3. Chapter contracts and context packages.
4. Market scans, candidate slates, reviews, quality debt, indexes, and reports.
5. Conversation previews.

Lower layers must not silently override higher layers.

## Book artifacts

### Discovery

`discovery/market-scan.yaml` stores dated, attributable demand signals. It informs selection but does not become canon.

`discovery/topic-candidates.yaml` compares three to eight original, serially sustainable topics against the same market, reader, and channel.

`discovery/topic-decision.yaml` records the selected candidate, rejected alternatives, accepted trade-off, reader-test hypothesis, and protected-originality rules.

`discovery/topic-selection-report.json` is generated by `novelctl topics`. Do not edit it manually. Its source fingerprints are part of the accepted brief fingerprint, so changed discovery evidence requires regeneration, invalidation, and brief re-approval.

### `author-intent.md`

Record the durable creative intent, desired reader experience, protected themes, and explicit no-go decisions.

### `planning/market-position.yaml`

Define the target platform (`fanqie` or `zhihu-salt`), work form (`long-serial` or `short-complete`), narrow target reader, distribution channel, publication format, repeatable reader promise, acceptable section/chapter-length range, testable commercial assumptions, and content boundaries.

This file, `novel-brief.md`, and the discovery artifacts form one accepted brief fingerprint. The selected candidate's platform, form, target reader, and channel must match this file exactly. A chapter contract must stay inside the accepted length range. Do not hide a positioning change inside an individual chapter contract.

### `current-focus.md`

Record the temporary focus for the next one to three chapters. Keep it replaceable; do not turn it into permanent canon.

### `planning/novel-brief.md`

Before approval, include:

- target reader and publication format;
- primary reader promise;
- protagonist desire and repeatable action loop;
- core conflict and escalation source;
- early hook;
- high-level ending direction;
- protected decisions and open questions.

### Foundation

`story-bible.md` defines the story engine and long-range promises.

`world-rules.yaml` stores stable rule IDs, constraints, costs, and forbidden crossings.

`characters/character-roster.md` indexes active characters. Put detailed recurring-character profiles in separate files and read only participating profiles per chapter.

### Volume planning

`volumes/current-volume.md` defines the volume promise, escalation ladder, midpoint shift, climax, payoff, and transition into the next volume. Plan the current volume more firmly than distant volumes.

## Artifact states

- `missing`: no usable artifact exists.
- `draft`: editable and not authoritative.
- `accepted`: approved or delegated; safe as an upstream dependency.
- `stale`: previously usable but invalidated by a changed dependency.

Never infer `accepted` from file existence alone.
Accepted artifacts store a SHA-256 fingerprint in `novel-state.yaml`. Editing an accepted source requires explicit invalidation and downstream re-approval.

## State changes

Use `novelctl` for `novel-state.yaml`. Do not edit state manually.

An invalid transition must leave the authoritative state unchanged. Generated run snapshots and event logs may remain as diagnostic evidence.
